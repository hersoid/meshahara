A Suliana
Indonesia
↳ Documentation

Flowers and foliage emerge from near-total darkness, a visual "root system" in which every element — petals, leaves, butterfly, the snake-like form — grows from the same dark root while retaining its own shape. This responds to the "Many Roots, One Ledger" theme: how individual identity can stay visible and intact while sharing the same visual space/infrastructure. The small butterfly in the lower corner represents the politics of visibility — a small entity, nearly invisible, yet present and entitled to its own space. The organic structure of the flowers and roots becomes a metaphor for distributed networks: every part connected, yet none uniform.

AlphaOmega8
@The_AO8
↳ Documentation

The work builds on the concept of identity and connections. Based on the advanced 3D reconstruction of the hemibrain of Drosophila melanogaster (the common fruit fly), released in 2020 by HHMI's Janelia Research Campus and Connectomics at Google. Biologists have used fruit flies as a model organism to infer human biological processes for over a century — despite obvious differences (including a genetic homology of just 60%) — for several key reasons, including conserved biological pathways, low genetic redundancy, and speed and scale properties.

The XYZ triptych is created from 20-micron thick slabs captured by focused ion beam scanning electron microscopes, stitched and aligned into a 26-trillion pixel 3D volume through computational methods. Digitally re-stained, modified and mounted on video, with AI music created using Mureka (full license). This artwork incorporates material from the "FlyEM Hemibrain Dataset" by the Janelia Research Campus / HHMI, used under CC BY 4.0. August 2026.

10
DOM
↳ Documentation

"Dom" (Home) is a triptych built around a private cipher: I sign my work using geometric shapes, where each letter of the alphabet corresponds to a form. The word "Dom" — Home — becomes triangle, circle, square. This is my authorship made visible as infrastructure: a personal ledger encoded directly into the surface of the work.

Each panel shows organic, root-like tendrils of light converging toward one of these three shapes at the centre — red toward the triangle, yellow-green toward the circle, blue-violet toward the square. The branching lines evoke both neural networks and root systems: distributed structures that carry signal, energy, and identity from many directions into a single point, without erasing where each strand began.

My cipher functions like a private key — visible, but only legible to those who know the system. It sits within the composition the way authorship sits within a blockchain: openly displayed, cryptographically distinct, impossible to counterfeit once you understand the code.

M808347
↳ Documentation (with Hooosney & Signifixance)

The work places the participant inside a fictional digital prison, where they encounter AI-driven characters and are invited into a "Digital Confession" — an intimate conversation about identity, vulnerability, fear, freedom, and human nature. The prison functions as both a narrative environment and a metaphor for contemporary digital life: a space in which we are constantly observed, recorded, classified, and asked to reveal ourselves.

This connects directly to Many Roots, One Ledger and its question of how we can participate in shared digital systems without surrendering identity. In Out of Jail, participation does not require the participant's private confession to become public. The project also explores coexistence between human and artificial identities. AI characters are not simply tools or interfaces; they exist as inhabitants of the same fictional world as the participant.

20
Mark Walhimer

Meshahara — to make public, to witness, to be seen. "Invisible Layer" is a machine built for that verb.

The work is driven by a layer that is never displayed. A hidden motion map is computed every frame, and its colour channels become the spatial coordinates of everything visible. The work that does the work is not the work you see. Whose labour is visible, and whose is structural and unseen, is the question the architecture asks by existing.

Each instance grows from a seed. The seed determines the work completely; identical code under a different seed produces a different creature. I author the rule and not the outcome. A ledger recording this work records a seed, not an image — authorship at the level of the generative rule rather than the artifact. Green scanners, red layers, and blue depth probes occupy one volume simultaneously through RGB-to-XYZ feedback. Each channel keeps its own identity while contributing to a single image; none is overwritten.

Rapha Mey
↳ Documentation (with Zizzy & Surreal Beam)

"VSLS (Vessels)" is a series of glass shapes made by code, shown as moving light patterns in real time. The light passes through each glass and bends, and the pattern it makes is what we see. Some of the glass shapes come from blending two shapes together; others are assembled by a small neural net.

Many Roots, One Ledger asks who gets seen, and on whose terms. In this piece, each glass stands in for a person. On its own, it's just an empty shape — a vessel. It only becomes visible when light passes through it and changes direction because of it. The light follows the same simple rule every time. But every glass bends it a little differently, because every glass has a different shape. So every pattern on the wall is different too. No one chooses the light that reaches them. But what they do with it is what makes them visible.

Stella Skopal
↳ Documentation

THE ROIDS, NIGHT JITTERS and PRIMORDIAL approach the exhibition's idea of "Many Roots, One Ledger" through generative systems: shared structures that allow individual identities to emerge rather than flattening them.

THE ROIDS begins with 27 films, each reduced to a distinct three-colour palette derived from its visual identity. They all enter the same generative system, but remain recognisable through colour and cultural memory — different sources circulating through one shared structure. NIGHT JITTERS uses Brownian motion to animate 305 particles imagined as strangers moving through a crowded Miami night. PRIMORDIAL approaches the same idea at a more fundamental level — unique numerical seeds derived from the base-4 Champernowne constant generate an evolving field.

Across all three works, code acts as shared infrastructure rather than a tool for producing one predetermined image. The rules are common; what emerges from them is not.

"organic systems as metaphors for distributed infrastructure" — I think AI would be one of the only things considered "new" in digital art. I am actually strongly against AI in practically every one of its forms. Whenever it is centralized, closed source, in the cloud, etc. I know it's poison for humanity and a massive attack surface against human freedom. However, decentralized, locally hosted, open source AI is like a ray of sunlight coming through the clouds in my current view.

Experimenting with decentralized AI in the creative process, viewing it as a liberated field condition of our modern existence, feels like a worthwhile exploration. At the moment I am working to apply that liberated technology to physical mediums, then back to the digital sphere, and on and on, creating a feedback loop, expanding the creative process using consumer level computer hardware and basic art supplies.

Zizzy
↳ Documentation (with Rapha Mey & Surreal Beam)

Created in 2026, "algorithm" is an animated GIF (here sent as a video file) that works on a loop. While the letters of the word appear scattered and changing their colours, a hand very similar to the ones found on a computer's system seems to move but not click through these letters. It's possible to also see some small icons in the background.

This piece talks about how a dominant system powered by an "algorithm" is capable of choosing and leading people to make pre-defined choices, not letting space for free will. Even being colourful and seeming harmless, the "algorithm" here is the voice of something more powerful dictating where we're supposed to look at.

Lampwrecked
USA
↳ Documentation

I make work the way the nervous system makes sense of the world: by transduction. A flame, a light trail, a gesture, a wave comes in as raw signal and gets rebuilt as structure. That translation is where I find the work. It's what gets kept, what gets lost, and what the system invents to fill the gaps.

I call my practice corpgen: generative work derived from corporeal forms. My background is in psychology and neuroscience, and I'm less interested in depicting bodies than in building systems that perceive the way a body does. They take in signal, sort it, and build a picture from it. Sometimes that's custom software reading contrast in a long-exposure photograph. Sometimes it's a synthesizer driven by mycelial growth or live wind data, or a laser-etched image that's been set on fire and turned back into data. In Ocean View, it's live surf cams from coastlines around the world, cut apart and rewoven. The sea's own measurements (wave height, period, swell direction) decide how much space each shoreline gets and how the image moves.

Working as Lampwrecked, I'm a New York–based artist and Assistant Director at Cycol Gallery.
